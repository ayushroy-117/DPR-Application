1. Gross profit — trading business
Applies to Electronics Trading & Retail. Your current formula is inverted — purchases are being added instead of subtracted from adjusted revenue.
Gross profit (correct formula)Fix this first
Step 1 — adjusted revenue (cost of goods available)
Adjusted Revenue = Sales + Closing Stock - Opening Stock
Step 2 — cost of goods sold (COGS)
COGS = Stock Purchases + Direct Wages + Direct Expenses
Step 3 — gross profit
Gross Profit = Adjusted Revenue - COGS
Your ERP bug: it computed (Sales + Closing Stock + Purchases - Opening Stock) which adds purchases to revenue. Fix: subtract COGS from adjusted revenue. With ₹10L sales and ₹22L purchases, this business runs at a gross LOSS unless gross margin % is applied to purchases, not full cost.
For a trading business, stock purchases should reflect only the cost of goods actually sold, not total inventory procured. Typical gross margin for electronics retail is 8–15%. If annual sales = ₹10,00,000 and gross margin = 12%, then COGS = ₹8,80,000 — not ₹22,00,000.
2. DSCR — Debt Service Coverage Ratio
Most critical ratio for PMEGP, PMFME and Swabalamban. Required ≥ 1.50 (PMEGP), ≥ 1.75 average (bank norms), minimum 1.0 in any single year.
DSCR formula (RBI / banking norms)Currently broken
Numerator — Net Cash Accrual
Net Cash Accrual = PAT + Depreciation
Denominator — Total Debt Service (annual)
Total Debt Service = TL Principal Repaid (year) + Interest on TL
DSCR for each year
DSCR (Year N) = (PAT + Depreciation) / (TL Principal + TL Interest)
Average DSCR (5 years)
Avg DSCR = SUM of all 5 year DSCRs / 5

Root cause: your ERP appears to be computing DSCR as a negative number, likely dividing (PAT - Depreciation - Interest) by debt service instead of (PAT + Depreciation). WC interest should NOT be in the denominator — only Term Loan principal + TL interest goes in the denominator.
Note: Interest on Working Capital (CC Loan) is an operating expense, already reflected in PAT. Never include it in the debt service denominator — this double-counts it and artificially suppresses DSCR.

Balance sheet formulasDoes not balance
Accounts payable (correct)
Accounts Payable = (Stock Purchases / 365) × Creditor Days Creditor Days for electronics retail = 30–45 days Example (Year 1): Stock Purchases = ₹8,80,000 (corrected COGS) Creditor Days = 30 Accounts Payable = (8,80,000 / 365) × 30 = ₹72,329

Reserves & surplus (cumulative)
Reserves (Year 1) = PAT (Year 1) - Drawings (Year 1) Reserves (Year N) = Reserves (Year N-1) + PAT (Year N) - Drawings (Year N)
Net fixed assets (WDV method)
Net Fixed Assets (Year 1) = Gross Block - Depreciation (Year 1) Net Fixed Assets (Year N) = Net Fixed Assets (Year N-1) - Depreciation (Year N)
Term loan balance
TL Balance (Year N) = TL Balance (Year N-1) - Principal Repaid (Year N)
Validation check (must equal zero)
Difference = Total Assets - Total Liabilities = (Net Fixed Assets + Inventory + Trade Receivables + Cash) - (Capital + Reserves + TL Balance + WC Loan + Accounts Payable) Must = 0. Block report generation if |Difference| > 1.

4. Cash flow — indirect method
Your chart showed cash going to -₹55L while the statement showed +₹55L. The chart was reading from a wrong data source.
Cash flow — indirect method (correct)Chart data mismatch
Operating cash flow
Operating CF = PAT + Depreciation [non-cash add-back] + Increase in Creditors [working capital source] - Increase in Debtors [working capital use] - Increase in Inventory [working capital use]
Financing cash flow
Financing CF = + WC Loan Drawn (Year 1 only) + Capital Introduced (Year 1 only) + TL Drawn (Year 1 only) - TL Principal Repaid - Drawings / Owner Withdrawals - Interest on TL [if not already in PAT]
Net cash flow & closing balance
Net CF (Year N) = Operating CF + Financing CF Closing Balance (Year N) = Opening Balance (Year N) + Net CF (Year N) Opening Balance (Year N) = Closing Balance (Year N-1) Opening Balance (Year 1) = 0
Your chart data: the Cash Balance Growth chart pulled a negative series — it was likely reading outflows only or using a sign-inverted variable. Closing Balance must always match the Cash Flow Statement's closing figure exactly.
